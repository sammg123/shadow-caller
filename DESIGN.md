# Design System: Shadow Play-Caller

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Stadium Is the Interface"**

The screen is a window into a football stadium on game night, and everything that reads is something a stadium already owns. The next call is on the video board. The plays step across the LED ribbon boards. The game score and the model's own score sit on a painted scoreboard, where SHADOW plays LEAGUE in the row under the game. The prediction is drawn on the field itself: before every caller snap the stadium lights dip and his two most likely plays appear on the turf as dashed, translucent shadow plays, in the same shapes the real plays take (a pass an arc, a run a straight line from the line of scrimmage), each with "1 · Deep shot (20+) · 31%" under it; at the snap the shadows fade and the real play draws solid with its label (docs/design/play-drawing.md, binding). Nothing on screen is interface chrome that a stadium would not own: no cards, no glass, no gradient panels, no floating tooltips.

This is an Operate surface carrying demo weight. Sam reads it between snaps on a laptop in a dark room, and Conor reads it from 8 to 14 feet on a meeting-room TV. So the layout follows stadium signage, which is built to be read from 100 yards: the field owns the middle; information sits at the edges where stadiums hang it (a ribbon across the top, the video board upper right, the lower ribbon and the scoreboard band across the bottom); every board holds one message at a time; and the next call is the brightest readout on screen. Density is moderate. Depth is real, because the bowl is a 3D scene, so the HUD itself stays flat, opaque and bolted in front of it like fixtures.

Light is the medium, and it is disciplined. Glow exists only where a physical fixture emits it (light towers, LED fascia boards, the ball's tracer). LED panels are opaque black with their unlit pixels visible, which makes them a material rather than a glow. LED copy is warm white and amber, never neon. Day games render in afternoon sun with the stadium's shadow line across the turf, so the world is not a night-only mood. Everything the feed cannot measure is drawn in coarse pixels or a checkerboard, so approximations look approximate. Rejected on purpose: the dark sports-analytics dashboard (near-black ground, neon accents, KPI cards, a flat green rectangle), its light data-journalism opposite, and the current cool-gray card UI.

**Key Characteristics:**

- A full-bleed 3D bowl under every pixel; the HUD is stadium hardware in front of it.
- Three materials only: LED panel (black, visible pixel grid, warm-white and amber light), painted steel (flat, opaque, 2 px corners), and the lit field.
- The offense paints the scoreboard: the band across the bottom is the team in possession's ESPN color.
- One message per board at a time; text steps in and holds, never crawls.
- Doto dot-matrix for big LED readouts only (40 px and up); Big Shoulders for every word.
- The signature moment is the shadow plays: lights dip, his top 2 appear on the turf as dashed shadows with their odds, the snap brings the lights back and draws the real play solid.

### Motion grammar

Native motion is what stadium hardware does between states. One authored moment per snap, driven by state; every item below becomes an instant state change under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`html[data-motion="reduced"]`), and the engine skips world moments there.

- **LED steps (12 fps).** Ribbon and board text changes in discrete frames of 83 ms (`--sc-dur-fast`): a new message steps in from the right in 5 frames (`steps(5, jump-end)`, 415 ms), holds, and the old one steps out left. Never a continuous crawl, never a smooth slide, never a crossfade.
- **Scanline fade-up (400 ms).** The video board's call appears row by row, top to bottom in 10 steps (`--sc-dur-slow`, `steps(10, jump-end)`). The shadow plays fade in over the same 400 ms (`look.motion.ghostInMs`) and out in 300 ms at the snap (`ghostOutMs`).
- **Light dip (20%, 300 ms).** At the pre-snap moment the stadium lights fall to 80% in 300 ms (`--sc-dur-med`) around the shadow plays, and come back to 100% in 300 ms at the snap. The LED boards and the two broadcast lines keep their brightness; only the stadium lights fall. Live games carry an OFF setting (Components, control-room drawer); Watch this game always dips unless motion is reduced.
- **Segment digits snap.** Scoreboard digits change with no tween. When a SHADOW v LEAGUE pair changes leader, its lamp relights with one 83 ms blink.
- **Camera by altitude.** Blimp holds the whole game, wide holds the drive, tight holds the snap. The snap pushes in toward tight (650 ms) and the ball is followed; after the play the camera settles back to wide (900 ms; a mode change eases over 1.1 s, `look.cameras.moveMs`). In Watch this game it holds 2 s on a touchdown or a turnover, rises to blimp between drives (1.7 s) and drifts there (2.4 degrees of yaw per second, at most 20, zoomed 1.65x, 42 degrees down, 25 yd up), then descends to wide for the next drive (1.1 s) (`look.world.uls.camera`).
- **Hardware moves like hardware.** The control-room drawer runs out on rails (240 ms, `--sc-ease-out`); steel never bounces, fades or scales. A possession change repaints the scoreboard band at once and moves the possession lamp; paint does not crossfade.

## Colors

Drenched: the surface is the stadium, colored by its own fixtures and paint. The night sky, the lit turf and the painted scoreboard are the big color fields; LED warm white and amber are the light; the offense's team color owns the scoreboard band.

### Primary
- **LED Warm White** (`led-warm-white`): the lit pixel. The next call, ribbon copy, every word on an LED panel, focus rings. 17.1:1 on `led-panel` (measured).
- **LED Amber** (`led-amber`, also `segment-lit`): the scoreboard's digits, percentages on the boards, the run family, lit keys, lamps that mean "on". 10.5:1 on `led-panel`; 7.3:1 lit against `segment-unlit`, so an unlit 8 stays readable as off (measured).
- **LED Gold** (`led-gold`): the midpoint of the warm ramp, used only for the RPO family, which is half of each (13.3:1 on `led-panel`). Pass, RPO and run read as three steps of one ramp: warm white, gold, amber. Labels always name the family; the ramp is a second cue, never the only one.

### Secondary
- **Scoreboard Steel** (runtime `--sc-team-off`): the offense's ESPN primary, exact, painted flat across the scoreboard band. It repaints when possession changes. With no team colors (manual mode before ESPN, DC C2) it is **Neutral Scoreboard Steel** (`scoreboard-steel-neutral`, white captions 10.4:1).
- **Caption White / Caption Dark** (`caption-white`, `caption-dark`): the paint for captions on team steel, white or black, whichever contrasts more (runtime `--sc-team-off-ink`, A10 `inkOn`). White on DAL navy is 14.1:1 and on TB red 6.2:1; the worst color in the gamut still gets 4.58:1 (measured), and captions are 19 px bold or larger, so every team passes.
- **Line to Gain Yellow** (`line-to-gain-yellow`): the line to gain on the turf, the penalty flag, the X key, warnings (retrying, the estimated-clock flag). 2.9:1 against `turf-mow-dark`, 13.2:1 on `led-panel`.
- **Line of Scrimmage Blue** (`line-of-scrimmage-blue`): the line of scrimmage, painted, and its meaning token. It reads by hue, not luminance (1.3:1 against the turf; every broadcast blue sits at 1.1 to 1.8:1, measured), so it is a full-width band of at least 0.4 yd with the white ball ring always on it.

### Tertiary
- **Turf** (`turf-mow-dark`, `turf-mow-light`): 5-yard mow stripes, albedo before light.
- **Field Paint** (`field-paint-white`): yard lines, numerals, hashes, sidelines; lit to about 0.85 linear so it never blooms (recipe pitfall 1).
- **Pylon Orange** (`pylon-orange`) and **Goalpost Gold** (`goalpost-gold`): the eight foam pylons and the posts, with orange ribbons on the uprights.
- **Light-Cone White** (`light-cone-white`): the color of the tower lights and of the lit field's falloff; also the light core mixed into every drawn path (see The Light Core Rule).
- **Day** (`day-sky`, `day-haze`, `afternoon-sun`): the afternoon variant for games that kick off before 5 pm local time at the venue (DC v1.2 `game.light`), rendered and in use: the sun at 2.6, a `sky-light-day` over `ground-light-day` hemisphere at 1.2, `day-haze` fog at density 0.0021.

### Neutral
- **Night Sky** (`night-sky`): the deep blue above the bowl and the page ground behind the canvas. Never black.
- **Bowl Haze** (`bowl-haze`): the exponential haze and the sky at the rim of the bowl (1.4:1 above the night sky, so the bowl's silhouette reads).
- **LED Panel** (`led-panel`): the face of every ribbon, board, digit window, input and toast. Opaque.
- **Unlit Pixel** (`led-unlit-pixel`, `#342E28`, 1.41:1 on the panel; G5-17 lifted it a step from `#2A2622` at 1.26:1) and **Unlit Segment** (`segment-unlit`, 1.43:1): the pixels and digit cells that are off. They are always visible; they are what makes LED a material. The grid survives a TV's or a screen recording's downscale: each unlit pixel sits inside one 2 x 2 block of screen pixels (the 4 px tile is offset 1 px and faces hang on the even pitch), so halving the picture keeps it instead of averaging it to flat black (`--uls-led-face`).
- **LED Dim** (`led-dim`): secondary LED copy (labels, league values, hints); 8.5:1 on the panel, 7.1:1 on steel.
- **Fascia Steel** (`fascia-steel`), **Raised Steel** (`fascia-steel-raised`), **Steel Seam** (`steel-seam`), **Steel Edge** (`steel-edge`), **Steel Frame** (`steel-frame`): the painted structure: board cabinets, the desk, the drawer, key caps, rules and the 2 px light-catch on top edges; the frame is the lighter steel of an LED window's surround (`--uls-steel-frame`). Steel is 1.17:1 against the night sky, so every cabinet carries its steel-edge top line.
- **Team Gray, Neutral** (`team-gray-neutral`): the neutral look's stand-in team color. It is never painted in this world: when `--sc-team-off` is still this gray (no ESPN colors yet), the band paints `scoreboard-steel-neutral` instead (a style query in `layout.css`).
- **Tally Red** (`tally-red`) and **Lamp Green** (`lamp-green`): lamp colors. Red is ESPN live (the on-air tally), errors and misses; green is better and hits. Both appear only as lamps, glyphs or text with a word beside them (5.8:1 and 11.0:1 on the panel).
- **Night Scrim** (`scrim-night`, 72%): the only translucent color, used once: under the stat board (G), over the bowl's free area only (`layout.css`), never over the HUD. The phone's sheets are opaque steel.

### Stadium materials

The bowl's own colors (frontmatter, after `team-gray-neutral`), all scene-only: never HUD paint and never LED light. The night stands are steel and concrete in vertex colors (`stand-*`: treads, risers, fascia, parapet, edge, back, end, the home stand's ribs, its lit concourse band and dark gates), lit by the tower spill and graded by the haze; by day the same stadium in sunlight (`stand-day-*`). The towers are dark steel (`tower-mast`, `tower-head`, `tower-brace`) with a black lamp face (`tower-face`; by day `tower-glass-day`). The ground outside the stands steps from `ground-night-1` at the bowl to `ground-night-9` at the horizon, with a lamp-lit pool (`ground-pool`); by day `ground-day-1` to `-9`. The crowd is 56,000 seeded points: both teams' primaries and alternates beside street clothes (`crowd-street-*`), skin tones (`crowd-skin-*`) and white shirts (`crowd-shirt-white`), at 25 to 60% brightness. The sky light is a hemisphere (`sky-light-night` over `ground-light-night` at 0.24; by day `sky-light-day` over `ground-light-day` at 1.2); the field's surround is `field-surround`; the ball is `ball-leather` with `field-paint-white` laces.

### Named Rules

**The Fixture Rule.** Light glows only where a physical fixture emits it: the tower lamps, the LED fascia ribbons in the bowl, and the ball's tracer. HUD panels, text, lines, the shadow plays and team colors never glow, never get a halo, never pulse.

**The Unlit Pixel Rule.** Every LED surface shows its off pixels: a 4 px pitch dot grid of `led-unlit-pixel` on `led-panel`, and an unlit `8` behind every digit cell. A black rectangle with glowing text is the rut; a panel with visible dark pixels is a board.

**The Offense Paints the Board Rule.** The team color is paint, never light. It covers the scoreboard band and the end zones; it never appears as LED text, an LED bar, a glow or a border on an LED panel (a team's own logo image, shown in an LED window, is the one exception). On LED, the caller is warm white and the opponent is amber.

**The Light Core Rule.** Every drawn path on the field (tracer trail, run lane, yards-after-catch stroke, return) has a light core, the team color mixed 65% toward `light-cone-white`, and carries the team color only on its edge or fill. TB red has the turf's luminance (1.03:1 on the contract's reference turf `#2e6b3a`, 1.49:1 on `turf-mow-dark`) and DAL navy vanishes at night (1.30:1 on `night-sky`), so a team color alone is never a signal.

**The No-Neon Rule.** LED hues are warm white, gold and amber; lamps are tally red and lamp green. No cyan, magenta, lime, electric violet or any fully saturated hue outside the team colors ESPN sends.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Big Shoulders Display (with Arial Narrow, sans-serif)
**Text Font:** Big Shoulders Text (with Arial Narrow, sans-serif)
**LED Font:** Doto, the variable dot-matrix face, at `wght` 800 and `ROND` 60 (fat round dots that nearly touch), behind two one-glyph patches of Big Shoulders Display (`unicode-range`): "ULS LED Punct" for U+002E and U+003A, so the decimal point and the clock's colon are square dots, and "ULS LED Pct" for U+0025 at `size-adjust: 58%` (G4-7), because Doto's own percent sign reads "28X" at ten feet and the full-size solid sign stood taller and darker than the open dot digits; at 58% it sits under the digits' height, a unit beside a number. The stack is `"ULS LED Punct", "ULS LED Pct", Doto` (`--uls-led-num`); ghost and lit text share it, so the unlit `88%` still sits cell for cell behind the lit figure

**Character:** Big Shoulders is condensed Chicago signage lettering: painted captions and the call in the Display cut, every label, button and line of copy in the Text cut. Doto is the board's own lettering for big numbers. All three are OFL and self-hosted under `static/fonts/under-the-lights/` (G28), latin subsets, each with its `OFL.txt`: Big Shoulders Display 35.5 KB, Big Shoulders Text 36.3 KB, Doto 6.0 KB (measured woff2 sizes). Canvas and turf text waits for `document.fonts.load()` before drawing (recipe pitfall 9).

Measured facts the build depends on:

- Doto is monospaced at 0.6 em per character on a 0.1 em dot grid (38.4 px per character at 64 px). The call names run to 23 characters ("PA intermediate (10–19)", "RPO, handed off or kept", 883 px at 64 px), so a call name never fits Doto at board size. The name is Big Shoulders Display; the percentage is Doto.
- Big Shoulders Display 800 in caps, tracking 0, sets every one of the 14 call names on one line: widest "RPO, HANDED OFF OR KEPT" 357.9 px at 40 px (1440 board measure 372 px), 483.1 px at 54 px (1920 measure 504 px), 286.3 px at 32 px (phone measure 322 px). Tracking of +0.01em would eat 9 of the 14 spare pixels at 1440, so the call name is set at 0.
- Big Shoulders has no tabular figures: `1111` sets at 41.8 px and `8888` at 73.8 px at 40 px, with or without `tnum`. Doto's digits are all 0.6 em.
- Doto draws its period and colon as plus-shaped dot clusters: `.421` reads as "+421" and `8:41` as "8‡41" (rendered and checked). The "ULS LED Punct" patch fixes both; ghost and lit text share the stack, so cells still align. Doto has no ▲ ▼ or block glyphs (they fall back to a system font), so hot arrows are drawn as pixel triangles.

### Named Rules

**The 40 Pixel Rule.** Doto appears at 40 px or larger, only, at every viewport. Below 40 px an LED says it in Big Shoulders Text, warm white or amber.

**The Whole Name Rule.** The call's name is never truncated, abbreviated, ellipsized or shrunk to fit; the board is sized to the measured widest name. If a future name breaks the measure, it wraps to a second line.

**The Ticking Number Rule.** A number that changes in place (the clock, scores, down, the call's percentage, the SHADOW v LEAGUE values) is Doto in a digit cell, so it never jitters; an unlit `8` sits behind every digit position (`88` behind a score, `88:88` behind the clock, `88%` behind a percentage), right-aligned, hidden from assistive tech (`content: "88" / ""`). The ghost sets the cell's width: stack the ghost and the value in one grid area (`display: inline-grid`, both `grid-area: 1 / 1`, `justify-self: end`), so a one-digit score sits in the right-hand cell with an unlit 8 beside it and nothing spills over the caption. Letter fields (down and distance words, the ball-on side) carry no ghost.

**The Caps Rule.** Painted captions and LED copy are set in caps with `text-transform`, from the sentence-case copy in `model/copy.js`; the source strings never change. ESPN's play text on the desk, the why lines, hints and toasts stay in sentence case.

## Elevation & Depth

The HUD is flat and the stadium is deep. Depth is physical: the bowl is a real 3D scene with real light, and the HUD is hardware mounted in front of the window. Panels separate from the scene by material (opaque LED black or painted steel), by a 2 px light-catch along their top edge (`steel-edge` on fascia steel; the band's own color mixed 30% toward white on the scoreboard), and by 1 px `steel-seam` lines between parts. There is no shadow vocabulary.

**The Flat Hardware Rule.** No `box-shadow`, drop shadow, `backdrop-filter`, blur, glass or decorative gradient on any HUD element. The only CSS gradient in the HUD is the unlit-pixel grid, which is texture, and the only spread-ring is the focus ring's 2 px `night-sky` gutter.

**The Opaque Panel Rule.** Every panel is opaque. The bowl shows through nothing except the stat board's scrim and the phone's sheets.

### The stadium (3D art direction)

- **The bowl.** Two decks of tiered stands as dark low-poly superellipse rings, one draw call (`stadium/bowl.js` RINGS, world yards): the lower deck 78 by 46 yd across, 28 rows (10 on the home side, under the camera), with a 2.6 yd parapet; the upper deck 100 by 68 yd, 22 rows, a horseshoe left open on the home side (1.12 rad) so the default camera sees over it. The home stand's back shows steel ribs every 7 yd and a lit concourse band (G3-2). A seeded crowd of 56,000 points tinted by both teams (Stadium materials), hidden below aspect 1. Two LED fascia ribbons, on the field wall all round and on the upper deck's front, are diegetic copies of the HUD ribbons (warm white and amber on LED black, `FrontSide` only, stepping at the same 12 fps). Four light towers (`look.js` TOWERS): the home pair behind the home sideline at x −46 and 146, 43 yd back, 50 yd high, the far pair 52 yd high; their lamp banks (9 by 4 lamps) are the brightest objects in the scene.
- **Night.** Each tower is a spot light in `light-cone-white` (intensity 29000, a 0.39 rad cone, penumbra 1) aimed across the field; only the two home-side towers cast shadows (2 shadow lights, maps 2048, PCF). A `sky-light-night` hemisphere at 0.24 and a room environment at 0.06 keep the stands from going black. `FogExp2` in `bowl-haze` at density 0.0028 softens the stands and towers (the field itself never takes fog) under a sky graded from `bowl-haze` at the rim to `night-sky` overhead. Top tier only: faint additive light shafts from each tower at 6% opacity, never above the bloom threshold.
- **Day.** A kickoff before 5 pm local time at the venue (DC v1.2 `game.light`; unknown counts as night): `afternoon-sun` as one directional light (2.6) from the west-southwest, low behind the far stands on the left, so the upper deck's rim lays its shadow line across the far part of the field; a `sky-light-day` hemisphere at 1.2; `day-sky` with `day-haze` fog at 0.0021; the stands in their day tones; tower lamps off, LED boards still lit. As built and in use.
- **The field.** The turf shader with 5-yard mow stripes in `turf-mow-dark` and `turf-mow-light` and every line in `field-paint-white` at 94% (roughness 0.84, a little grain); each end zone in its defending team's exact ESPN primary, the team name painted in white (or the alternate) with an outline; numerals and hashes; eight `pylon-orange` pylons; `goalpost-gold` single-standard posts with orange ribbons; the field's surround in `field-surround`.
- **The lines.** The line of scrimmage (`line-of-scrimmage-blue`) and the line to gain (`line-to-gain-yellow`) are paint on the turf, 0.45 yd wide, coplanar through `polygonOffset`, with no halo and no bloom; the line to gain hides on goal to go. The ball rests on the line of scrimmage, mid-field (ESPN gives no hash; the field legend says so).
- **Bloom.** `UnrealBloomPass` at half resolution, threshold 1.05, strength 0.55, radius 0.5, then the world's lens pass (`uls-lens`); Neutral tone mapping at exposure 1.0. Only the tower lamps, the LED fascia and the ball's tracer head exceed 1.0. White paint sits near 0.85 linear; the shadow plays stay below 1.0.
- **Cameras by altitude.** The camera's height says how much of the game you are looking at. V cycles them; FOV is vertical (`PerspectiveCamera.fov`); a mode change eases over 1.1 s (`inOutCubic`).

| Mode | Holds | Rig (`look.cameras`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wide` (default) | the drive: line of scrimmage, line to gain, the shadow plays, the first ring of stands | home sideline, 38 yd up, 82 yd back, 20 degrees |
| `tight` | the snap: the ball and the yards around it | sideline, 10 yd up, 40 yd back, 18 degrees, follows the ball |
| `blimp` | the game: the whole field and the bowl | 300 yd up, 140 yd back, 30 degrees; drifts between drives in Watch this game (Motion grammar) |

- **Team colors in the scene.** End zones keep the exact primary with white or alternate lettering and a white outline. Every path follows The Light Core Rule. The shadow plays are never team-colored.
- **Budget** (ui-architecture 6.1, 21.4). At most 150 draw calls and 300k triangles per frame, props at most 40 draw calls, 2 shadow-casting lights, bloom at half resolution, render on demand (0 frames while nothing moves; the blimp drift keeps the loop alive only while it runs), 60 fps at 1440x900 and 1920x1080 on Sam's laptop. The stadium is 5 draw calls (4 by day, 3 on a software rasterizer) and about 38k triangles with the field. Quality tiers drop the light shafts first, then the crowd, then bloom; a software rasterizer gets the low tier (DPR 1, no MSAA, no shadows, no bloom).
- **Room lights** (`html[data-ambient]`, the drawer's switch). `dark` is the default. `room` (a lit meeting room or a projector) lifts the scene's exposure by 0.5 EV (`field/lighting.js` `ROOM_EV`, `exposureFor`), so a projector does not crush the bowl to mud; the HUD stays the same. The switch hands the change to the app first (a cancelable `sc:booth-ambient` event) and keeps `sc:ambient` itself, so it works either way.
- **The 2D fallback** (no WebGL2, a lost context, `?scene=2d`) is the same world seen from above: a `night-sky` ground, the striped field with team end zones, the same plays, shadow plays and labels in SVG, and the same HUD. Its shadow plays are drawn at the field's scale, so on the full-field view they are small (a known item for the 2D owner).

## Shapes

- **LED faces:** square corners (`rounded.led`, 0 px). A face is a rectangle on the 4 px pitch, and its edge is where the pixel grid ends; it has no border of its own.
- **Steel:** 2 px corners (`rounded.steel`), folded sheet metal, never more. Cabinets are rectangles with a caption plate on top and an 8 px frame around the face.
- **Hairline:** 1 px corners (`rounded.hairline`), only on parts a few pixels tall in the shared component CSS: the timeout pips (`scoreboard.css` `.sc-sb-to`, 14 x 4 px) and the scrub track (`scrub.css`). A 2 px corner would round a 4 px pip into a pill.
- **Lamps:** perfect circles, 10 px at 1440 and 12 px at 1920 (`rounded.lamp`), always paired with a word. With the ball's ring, the only round things on screen.
- **Pixel glyphs:** results (TD, INC, 1ST, FLAG, turnover, the check, the X) and the hot arrows on a 9 by 9 pixel grid, lit in the LED palette; referee signal pictograms, drawn as pixel art, on the video board for touchdowns and turnovers only.
- **Strokes on the turf:** a play is an arc through the air or a straight flat line on the turf (play-drawing.md); a shadow play is the same shape, dashed. The 1-yard pixel grid of the seed's projection was replaced by these shapes in v1.3.
- **Refused shapes:** pills, rounded cards, cards inside cards, cut corners and slanted ends (broadcast graphics), icon tiles, circles used as decoration.

### The shadow plays (signature)

- **When:** before every caller snap, while a prediction is up (`data-phase="presnap"`). Opponent snaps get none (the opponent has no model). In Watch this game the call is up for at least 2 s before every Dallas snap up to 4x and about 1.5 s at 10x (data contract E3).
- **Sequence:** the stadium lights dip to 80% (300 ms; `look.world.uls.dip`) → his top 2 plays fade onto the turf (400 ms) with the board's call scanning in → they hold until the snap → at the snap the lights come back (300 ms), the shadows fade out (300 ms) and the real play draws solid with its label. With the live OFF setting there is no dip; under reduced motion everything is instant and there is no dip.
- **Look (v1.3, play-drawing.md 4; G3-1):** his top 2 specific plays by probability, in the same shape language as real plays, from the line of scrimmage: a pass an arc to the middle lane at its bucket's typical air yards (short 6, intermediate 14, deep 25; play-action the same), a screen a short arc to the flat on the wider side, an RPO handoff a line and an RPO throw a short arc, a run a straight line about 4 yd long angled to its gap (inside straight ahead, off-tackle about 4 yd wide, outside about 9). Dashed (1.6 on, 0.7 off) and translucent: 50% for #1, 35% for #2 (on 4th down, "if he goes for it", 35% and 25%). Tinted by family on the board's ramp (pass `led-warm-white`, run `led-amber`, RPO `led-gold`), each over a dark casing (`night-sky` at 40%, 1.8 wide) so it reads on the lit turf; never team-colored, never solid.
- **Text:** under each shadow, "1 · Deep shot (20+) · 31%" and "2 · Inside run (A/B gap) · 22%" in the engine's leaf names, stacked so they never overlap each other or the strokes.
- **Linked:** focus or hover on a board row lights that play's shadow (90%) and dims the others (45%); a play outside the top 2 gets a shadow while hovered. The shadows never change color with the verdict.

### Plays on the field

The binding grammar is docs/design/play-drawing.md; in short:

- **Every play starts on the line of scrimmage**, on the center line between the hashes (ESPN gives no hash). No dropback, handoff mesh or fake. Kicks start on the line too (DECIDED, v1.4); a field goal's label keeps the true distance ("FG 41 yds · good").
- **Shapes:** a completed pass is an arc to the catch (a small catch dot), then a straight line on the turf for the yards after the catch; an incompletion is the arc ending in an X; an interception the arc to the spot, an INT mark, then the return in the defense's color; a run, scramble or kneel a straight flat line angled to its gap with its yards; a sack a line backward in a loss color; punts, kickoffs and field goals arcs at their true length with their result marks; a touchdown ends in the end zone with a TD mark.
- **Flags:** a play wiped out by a flag draws dimmed and dashed (38%), a flag at the line, and the ball walks to the enforced spot; a flag with no play (also one typed by hand, v1.4) is the flag at the line and the walk.
- **The label:** every play carries one, near its end and never over the ball: "Deep pass left · +21", "Inside run (up the middle) · +4", "Punt · 53 yds · out of bounds", "No play · defensive offside on TB", "Flag · false start on DAL · −5" (a true minus sign). Labels are text, never HTML.
- **Paths:** a light core (`light-cone-white`) with the team color on its edge (The Light Core Rule): arcs 4.5 px, lines on the turf 0.62 yd, marks 2.4 yd; an estimate (an incompletion's "short" or "deep" depth, a loose ball's runback) is dashed, a measured fact solid.
- **Persistence:** the last play stays, dimmed to 30%, until the next snap draws; the drive's earlier plays stay as residue (16%) until the drive ends.
- **Ball:** the football at 2.2x scale with a tracer head and a spark trail in the team color that fades in 400 ms; the head is the only blooming thing on the field.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** keep a stadium behind every pixel: the `night-sky` ground and the lit bowl at night, `day-sky` and sun in the afternoon.
- **Do** make every LED surface show its unlit pixels (4 px grid of `led-unlit-pixel`) and every digit cell its unlit 8s.
- **Do** keep panels opaque and flat: LED black or painted steel, a 2 px light-catch on top, 1 px seams.
- **Do** paint the scoreboard band in the offense's exact ESPN primary and repaint it on every possession change.
- **Do** set the call's name on one line in Big Shoulders Display (40 px at 1440, 54 px at 1920) and its percentage in Doto (64 px, 88 px); the call is the brightest, largest readout on screen.
- **Do** put a light core in every drawn path and carry team colors only on edges and fills.
- **Do** draw every estimate dashed and every measured fact solid; draw the shadow plays dashed and translucent, never solid.
- **Do** show only pairs the scorecard computes on the SHADOW v LEAGUE row: called right, actual play in his top 3, pass vs run log loss.
- **Do** step LED text in at 12 fps and hold it; fade the shadow plays and scan the call in over 400 ms; snap digits; make every motion instant under reduced motion.
- **Do** honor the live-game light-dip OFF setting, and check every screen at 1440x900, 1920x1080 and 390x844 with real copy, including the longest call name ("RPO, handed off or kept").

### Don't:

- **Don't** use a black or near-black flat UI ground, a neon hue, or a glowing edge: that is the dark analytics dashboard this world was chosen against.
- **Don't** give anything a glow, halo, bloom or pulse unless it is a physical fixture (tower lamps, LED fascia in the bowl) or the ball's tracer.
- **Don't** use `box-shadow` (the focus ring's gutter is the one exception), `backdrop-filter`, glass, blur, decorative gradients, pills, rounded cards or nested cards anywhere in the HUD.
- **Don't** set Doto below 40 px, type ▲ ▼ in Doto, or put a ticking number in Big Shoulders (no tabular figures).
- **Don't** truncate, abbreviate or ellipsize the call's name.
- **Don't** put team colors on LED (text, bars, glows; a team's own logo image is the one exception) or use a team color as the only signal of a path.
- **Don't** invent a statistic: no league top-pick count, no league tilt count, and no more precision than the scorecard shows (whole percentages, 3 decimals for pass vs run log loss, 2 for the others).
- **Don't** draw players, routes, formations, a dropback or a handoff mesh, or a side for a pass prediction: a shadow play is a shape from the line of scrimmage, nothing more.
- **Don't** crawl ribbon text continuously, crossfade boards, or animate paint.
- **Don't** let any panel cover the line of scrimmage, the line to gain or the shadow plays: the camera's safe area keeps them in the free field.
