# Design System: Shadow Play-Caller

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Stadium Is the Interface"**

The screen is a window into a football stadium on game night, and everything that reads is something a stadium already owns. The next call is on the video board, between his previous call and his last six. The newest play steps across the LED ribbon at the top. The game score sits on a painted scoreboard. The prediction is drawn on the field itself: before every caller snap the stadium lights dip and his two most likely plays appear on the turf as thin dashed shadow plays, in the same shapes the real plays take (a pass an arc, a run a straight line from the line of scrimmage), NEXT 1 in amber and NEXT 2 in warm white, each with its odds, his previous play beside them in steel; at the snap the shadows fade and the real play draws solid with its label (docs/design/play-drawing.md, binding). The model's own score, SHADOW v LEAGUE, hangs on a second page, the Model Report, built from the same hardware. Nothing on screen is interface chrome that a stadium would not own: no cards, no glass, no gradient panels, no floating tooltips.

This is a watch surface carrying demo weight. Sam drives it from the top bar's keys between snaps on a laptop in a dark room, and Conor reads it from 8 to 14 feet on a meeting-room TV. Nothing on it enters the game: the keys play, pause, step, seek and go live through what ESPN shows (restructure-v2.md 1). The GAME key opens any 2026 game, shadowing one side's play-caller or both, and ASK opens a local analyst that answers typed questions from the game's own numbers; neither enters a play. The layout follows stadium signage, which is built to be read from 100 yards: the field owns the middle; information sits at the edges where stadiums hang it (the top bar across the top, the video board upper right, the lower ribbon and the scoreboard band across the bottom); each ribbon holds one message at a time; and the next call is the brightest readout on screen. Density is moderate. Depth is real, because the bowl is a 3D scene, so the HUD itself stays flat, opaque and bolted in front of it like fixtures.

Light is the medium, and it is disciplined. Glow exists only where a physical fixture emits it (light towers, LED fascia boards, the ball's tracer). LED panels are opaque black with their unlit pixels visible, which makes them a material rather than a glow. LED copy is warm white and amber, never neon. Day games render in afternoon sun with the stadium's shadow line across the turf, so the world is not a night-only mood. Everything the feed cannot measure is drawn dashed (an incompletion's estimated depth, a loose ball's runback, every prediction), so approximations look approximate. Rejected on purpose: the dark sports-analytics dashboard (near-black ground, neon accents, KPI cards, a flat green rectangle), its light data-journalism opposite, and the current cool-gray card UI.

**Key Characteristics:**

- A full-bleed 3D bowl under every pixel; the HUD is stadium hardware in front of it.
- Three materials only: LED panel (black, visible pixel grid, warm-white and amber light), painted steel (flat, opaque, 2 px corners), and the lit field.
- The offense paints the scoreboard: the band across the bottom is the team in possession's ESPN color.
- One message per ribbon; the video board is one card with every section up at once; text steps in and holds, never crawls.
- Doto dot-matrix for big LED readouts only (40 px and up); Big Shoulders for every word.
- The signature moment is the shadow plays: lights dip, his top 2 appear on the turf as thin dashed NEXT 1 and NEXT 2 with their odds beside his LAST play in steel, the snap brings the lights back and draws the real play solid.
- No input into the game on screen: the top bar's keys only move through what ESPN shows. The two text fields find a game (the picker's pasted link) or ask about it (the analyst's question).
- With both sides tracked the stadium follows the ball: the board, the shadow plays and the analyst take the caller whose offense has it.
- The model's score lives on the Model Report, the same LED, steel and segment hardware on a page of its own.

### Motion grammar

Native motion is what stadium hardware does between states. One authored moment per snap, driven by state; every item below becomes an instant state change under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`html[data-motion="reduced"]`), and the engine skips world moments there.

- **LED steps (12 fps).** Ribbon and board text changes in discrete frames of 83 ms (`--sc-dur-fast`): a new message steps in from the right in 5 frames (`steps(5, jump-end)`, 415 ms), holds, and the old one steps out left. Never a continuous crawl, never a smooth slide, never a crossfade.
- **Scanline fade-up (400 ms).** The video board's call appears row by row, top to bottom in 10 steps (`--sc-dur-slow`, `steps(10, jump-end)`); a new previous play scans in the same way, and so do the specific plays and why lines when the card opens under the pointer. The shadow plays fade in over the same 400 ms (`look.motion.ghostInMs`) and out in 300 ms at the snap (`ghostOutMs`); his previous play dims to steel over those 400 ms as they come up.
- **Light dip (20%, 300 ms).** At the pre-snap moment the stadium lights fall to 80% in 300 ms (`--sc-dur-med`) around the shadow plays, and come back to 100% in 300 ms at the snap. The LED boards and the two broadcast lines keep their brightness; only the stadium lights fall. Live games carry an OFF setting (Components, settings menu); a watched game always dips unless motion is reduced.
- **Lamp flare (115%, 300 ms).** The tower lamp banks flare when he is right (a top pick) and on a touchdown (G3-7). Fixtures only, never the HUD.
- **Segment digits snap.** Scoreboard digits change with no tween. When a SHADOW v LEAGUE pair on the Model Report changes leader, its lamp relights with one 83 ms blink.
- **The camera is the viewer's.** Two views, Wide and Blimp, toggled with V (restructure-v2.md 4); a change eases over 1.1 s (`look.cameras.moveMs`, `inOutCubic`). Wide slides sideways with the ball while a play runs. The camera never moves on its own: no push-in at the snap, no rise between drives, no drift.
- **Hardware moves like hardware.** The settings menu and the game picker are there or put away, with no animation; steel never bounces, fades or scales. The one moving cabinet is the analyst's drawer, which runs out on its rails once as it opens (240 ms, `--sc-ease-out`; instant under reduced motion). A possession change repaints the scoreboard band at once and moves the possession lamp; paint does not crossfade.

## Colors

Drenched: the surface is the stadium, colored by its own fixtures and paint. The night sky, the lit turf and the painted scoreboard are the big color fields; LED warm white and amber are the light; the offense's team color owns the scoreboard band.

### Primary
- **LED Warm White** (`led-warm-white`): the lit pixel. The next call, ribbon copy, every word on an LED panel, focus rings, the TOP PICK verdict, his #2 shadow play and its NEXT 2 tab. 17.1:1 on `led-panel` (measured).
- **LED Amber** (`led-amber`, also `segment-lit`): the scoreboard's digits, percentages on the boards, the run family, the IN HIS TOP 3 verdict, lit keys, lamps that mean "on", the progress bar's played cells, his #1 shadow play and its NEXT 1 tab. 10.5:1 on `led-panel`; 7.3:1 lit against `segment-unlit`, so an unlit 8 stays readable as off (measured).
- **LED Gold** (`led-gold`): the midpoint of the warm ramp, used only for the RPO family, which is half of each (13.3:1 on `led-panel`). Pass, RPO and run read as three steps of one ramp: warm white, gold, amber. Labels always name the family; the ramp is a second cue, never the only one.

### Secondary
- **Scoreboard Steel** (runtime `--sc-team-off`): the offense's ESPN primary, exact, painted flat across the scoreboard band. It repaints when possession changes. With no team colors yet (before ESPN's summary arrives, DC C2) and at the final whistle it is **Neutral Scoreboard Steel** (`scoreboard-steel-neutral`, white captions 10.4:1).
- **Caption White / Caption Dark** (`caption-white`, `caption-dark`): the paint for captions on team steel, white or black, whichever contrasts more (runtime `--sc-team-off-ink`, A10 `inkOn`). White on DAL navy is 14.1:1 and on TB red 6.2:1; the worst color in the gamut still gets 4.58:1 (measured), and captions are 19 px bold or larger, so every team passes.
- **Line to Gain Yellow** (`line-to-gain-yellow`): the line to gain on the turf, the penalty flag, warnings (reconnecting, the estimated-clock flag, the card's 4th-down caveat). 2.9:1 against `turf-mow-dark`, 13.2:1 on `led-panel`.
- **Last Play Slate** (`last-play-slate`): his previous play's stroke and its LAST plate while his next call is up (play-drawing.md 4b): `steel-frame` mixed 40% toward `led-warm-white`, a muted steel that is never amber or warm white, so the last play never reads as a prediction. Its edge is `steel-edge`.
- **Line of Scrimmage Blue** (`line-of-scrimmage-blue`): the line of scrimmage, painted, and its meaning token. It reads by hue, not luminance (1.3:1 against the turf; every broadcast blue sits at 1.1 to 1.8:1, measured), so it is a full-width band of at least 0.4 yd with the white ball ring always on it.

### Tertiary
- **Turf** (`turf-mow-dark`, `turf-mow-light`): 5-yard mow stripes, albedo before light.
- **Field Paint** (`field-paint-white`): yard lines, numerals, hashes, sidelines; lit to about 0.85 linear so it never blooms (recipe pitfall 1).
- **Pylon Orange** (`pylon-orange`) and **Goalpost Gold** (`goalpost-gold`): the eight foam pylons and the posts, with orange ribbons on the uprights.
- **Light-Cone White** (`light-cone-white`): the color of the tower lights and of the lit field's falloff; also the light core mixed into every drawn path (see The Light Core Rule).
- **Day** (`day-sky`, `day-haze`, `afternoon-sun`): the afternoon variant for games that kick off before 5 pm local time at the venue (DC v1.2 `game.light`), rendered and in use: the sun at 2.6, a `sky-light-day` over `ground-light-day` hemisphere at 1.2, `day-haze` fog at density 0.0021.

### Neutral
- **Night Sky** (`night-sky`): the deep blue above the bowl and the page ground behind the canvas. Never black.
- **Bowl Haze** (`bowl-haze`): the exponential haze and the sky at the rim of the bowl (1.4:1 above the night sky, so the bowl's silhouette reads).
- **LED Panel** (`led-panel`): the face of every ribbon, board, digit window, the progress bar and every toast. Opaque.
- **Unlit Pixel** (`led-unlit-pixel`, `#342E28`, 1.41:1 on the panel; G5-17 lifted it a step from `#2A2622` at 1.26:1) and **Unlit Segment** (`segment-unlit`, 1.43:1): the pixels and digit cells that are off. They are always visible; they are what makes LED a material. The grid survives a TV's or a screen recording's downscale: each unlit pixel sits inside one 2 x 2 block of screen pixels (the 4 px tile is offset 1 px and faces hang on the even pitch), so halving the picture keeps it instead of averaging it to flat black (`--uls-led-face`).
- **LED Dim** (`led-dim`): secondary LED copy (labels, league values, hints); 8.5:1 on the panel, 7.1:1 on steel.
- **Fascia Steel** (`fascia-steel`), **Raised Steel** (`fascia-steel-raised`), **Steel Seam** (`steel-seam`), **Steel Edge** (`steel-edge`), **Steel Frame** (`steel-frame`): the painted structure: board cabinets, the top bar's playback row and nameplate, the settings menu, key caps, plates, rules and the 2 px light-catch on top edges; the frame is the lighter steel of an LED window's surround and a lamp's socket (`--uls-steel-frame`). Steel is 1.17:1 against the night sky, so every cabinet carries its steel-edge top line.
- **Team Gray, Neutral** (`team-gray-neutral`): the neutral look's stand-in team color. It is never painted in this world: when `--sc-team-off` is still this gray (no ESPN colors yet), the band paints `scoreboard-steel-neutral` instead (a style query in `layout.css`). At the final whistle the band is that neutral steel too: the game has no offense.
- **Tally Red** (`tally-red`) and **Lamp Green** (`lamp-green`): lamp colors. Red is the live edge (the on-air tally on Go Live and on the Model Report's status), errors and misses; green is better and hits. Both appear only as lamps, glyphs or text with a word beside them (5.8:1 and 11.0:1 on the panel).
- **Night Scrim** (`scrim-night`, 72%): the only translucent color. Every world defines it (`--sc-scrim`), but nothing in this world paints it since the stat board left the GameCast: no panel lets the bowl show through.

### Stadium materials

The bowl's own colors (frontmatter, after `team-gray-neutral`), all scene-only: never HUD paint and never LED light. The night stands are steel and concrete in vertex colors (`stand-*`: treads, risers, fascia, parapet, edge, back, end, the home stand's ribs, its lit concourse band and dark gates), lit by the tower spill and graded by the haze; by day the same stadium in sunlight (`stand-day-*`). The towers are dark steel (`tower-mast`, `tower-head`, `tower-brace`) with a black lamp face (`tower-face`; by day `tower-glass-day`). ### Named Rules

**The Fixture Rule.** Light glows only where a physical fixture emits it: the tower lamps, the LED fascia ribbons in the bowl, and the ball's tracer. HUD panels, text, lines, the shadow plays and team colors never glow, never get a halo, never pulse.

**The Unlit Pixel Rule.** Every LED surface shows its off pixels: a 4 px pitch dot grid of `led-unlit-pixel` on `led-panel`, and an unlit `8` behind every digit cell. A black rectangle with glowing text is the rut; a panel with visible dark pixels is a board.

**The Offense Paints the Board Rule.** The team color is paint, never light. It covers the scoreboard band and the end zones; it never appears as LED text, an LED bar, a glow or a border on an LED panel (a team's own logo image, shown in an LED window, is the one exception). On LED, the caller is warm white and the opponent is amber.

**The Light Core Rule.** Every drawn path on the field (tracer trail, run lane, yards-after-catch stroke, return) has a light core, the team color mixed 65% toward `light-cone-white`, and carries the team color only on its edge or fill. TB red has the turf's luminance (1.03:1 on the contract's reference turf `#2e6b3a`, 1.49:1 on `turf-mow-dark`) and DAL navy vanishes at night (1.30:1 on `night-sky`), so a team color alone is never a signal.

**The No-Neon Rule.** LED hues are warm white, gold and amber; lamps are tally red and lamp green. No cyan, magenta, lime, electric violet or any fully saturated hue outside the team colors ESPN sends.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Big Shoulders Display (with Arial Narrow, sans-serif)
**Text Font:** Big Shoulders Text (with Arial Narrow, sans-serif)
**LED Font:** Doto, the variable dot-matrix face, at `wght` 800 and `ROND` 60 (fat round dots that nearly touch), behind two one-glyph patches of Big Shoulders Display (`unicode-range`): "ULS LED Punct" for U+002E and U+003A, so the decimal point and the clock's colon are square dots, and "ULS LED Pct" for U+0025 at `size-adjust: 58%` (G4-7), because Doto's own percent sign reads "28X" at ten feet and the full-size solid sign stood taller and darker than the open dot digits; at 58% it sits under the digits' height, a unit beside a number. The stack is `"ULS LED Punct", "ULS LED Pct", Doto` (`--uls-led-num`); ghost and lit text share it, so the unlit `88%` still sits cell for cell behind the lit figure

**Character:** Big Shoulders is condensed Chicago signage lettering: painted captions and the call in the Display cut, every label, button and line of copy in the Text cut. Doto is the board's own lettering for big numbers. All three are OFL and self-hosted under `static/fonts/under-the-lights/` (G28), latin subsets, each with its `OFL.txt`: Big Shoulders Display 35.5 KB, Big Shoulders Text 36.3 KB, Doto 6.0 KB (measured woff2 sizes). Canvas and turf text waits for `document.fonts.load()` before drawing (recipe pitfall 9).

Measured facts the build depends on:

- Doto is monospaced at 0.6 em per character on a 0.1 em dot grid (38.4 px per character at 64 px). The call names run to 23 characters ("PA intermediate (10–19)", "RPO, handed off or kept", 883 px at 64 px), so a call name never fits Doto at board size. The name is Big Shoulders Display; the percentage is Doto.
- Big Shoulders Display 800 in caps, tracking 0, sets every one of the 14 call names on one line: widest "RPO, HANDED OFF OR KEPT" 357.9 px at 40 px (1440 board measure 372 px), 483.1 px at 54 px (1920 measure 504 px), 286.3 px at 32 px (phone measure 322 px). Tracking of +0.01em would eat 9 of the 14 spare pixels at 1440, so the call name is set at 0.
- Big Shoulders has no tabular figures: `1111` sets at 41.8 px and `8888` at 73.8 px at 40 px, with or without `tnum`. Doto's digits are all 0.6 em.
- Doto draws its period and colon as plus-shaped dot clusters: `.421` reads as "+421" and `8:41` as "8‡41" (rendered and checked). The "ULS LED Punct" patch fixes both; ghost and lit text share the stack, so cells still align. Doto has no ▲ ▼ or block glyphs (they fall back to a system font), so hot arrows are drawn as pixel triangles.

### Named Rules

**The 40 Pixel Rule.** Doto appears at 40 px or larger, only, at every viewport. Below 40 px an LED says it in Big Shoulders Text, warm white or amber.

**The Whole Name Rule.** The call's name is never truncated, abbreviated, ellipsized or shrunk to fit; the board is sized to the measured widest name. If a future name breaks the measure, it wraps to a second line.

**The Ticking Number Rule.** A number that changes in place (the clock, scores, down, the call's percentage, the SHADOW v LEAGUE values) is Doto in a digit cell, so it never jitters; an unlit `8` sits behind every digit position (`88` behind a score, `88:88` behind the clock, `88%` behind a percentage), right-aligned, hidden from assistive tech (`content: "88" / ""`). The ghost sets the cell's width: stack the ghost and the value in one grid area (`display: inline-grid`, both `grid-area: 1 / 1`, `justify-self: end`), so a one-digit score sits in the right-hand cell with an unlit 8 beside it and nothing spills over the caption. Letter fields (down and distance words, the ball-on side) carry no ghost.

**The Caps Rule.** Painted captions and LED copy are set in caps with `text-transform`, from the sentence-case copy in `model/copy.js` (and `report/model/copy.js`); the source strings never change. The then-line's play names, the split's labels, the why lines, notes, hints and toasts stay in sentence case; ESPN's own text rides on tooltips.

## Elevation & Depth

The HUD is flat and the stadium is deep. Depth is physical: the bowl is a real 3D scene with real light, and the HUD is hardware mounted in front of the window. Panels separate from the scene by material (opaque LED black or painted steel), by a 2 px light-catch along their top edge (`steel-edge` on fascia steel; the band's own color mixed 30% toward white on the scoreboard), and by 1 px `steel-seam` lines between parts. There is no shadow vocabulary.

**The Flat Hardware Rule.** No `box-shadow`, drop shadow, `backdrop-filter`, blur, glass or decorative gradient on any HUD element. The only CSS gradient in the HUD is the unlit-pixel grid, which is texture, and the only spread-ring is the focus ring's 2 px `night-sky` gutter.

**The Opaque Panel Rule.** Every panel is opaque, the settings menu, the game picker and the analyst's drawer included. The bowl shows through nothing.

### The stadium (3D art direction)

- **Day.** A kickoff before 5 pm local time at the venue (DC v1.2 `game.light`; unknown counts as night): `afternoon-sun` as one directional light (2.6) from the west-southwest, low behind the far stands on the left, so the upper deck's rim lays its shadow line across the far part of the field; a `sky-light-day` hemisphere at 1.2; `day-sky` with `day-haze` fog at 0.0021; the stands in their day tones; tower lamps off, LED boards still lit. As built and in use.
- **The field.** The turf shader with 5-yard mow stripes in `turf-mow-dark` and `turf-mow-light` and every line in `field-paint-white` at 94% (roughness 0.84, a little grain); each end zone in its defending team's exact ESPN primary, the team name painted in white (or the alternate) with an outline; numerals and hashes; eight `pylon-orange` pylons; `goalpost-gold` single-standard posts with orange ribbons; the field's surround in `field-surround`.
- **The lines.** The line of scrimmage (`line-of-scrimmage-blue`) and the line to gain (`line-to-gain-yellow`) are paint on the turf, 0.45 yd wide, coplanar through `polygonOffset`, with no halo and no bloom; the line to gain hides on goal to go. The ball rests on the line of scrimmage, mid-field (ESPN gives no hash; the field legend says so).
- **Bloom.** `UnrealBloomPass` at half resolution, threshold 1.05, strength 0.55, radius 0.5, then the world's lens pass (`uls-lens`); Neutral tone mapping at exposure 1.0. Only the tower lamps, the LED fascia and the ball's tracer head exceed 1.0. White paint sits near 0.85 linear; the shadow plays stay below 1.0.
- **Two cameras, the viewer's** (restructure-v2.md 4). The camera's height says how much of the game you are looking at. V toggles them and the choice is kept per viewer (`sc:camera`); FOV is vertical (`PerspectiveCamera.fov`); a change eases over 1.1 s (`inOutCubic`). No Tight view, and no move of the camera's own.

| Mode | Holds | Rig (`look.cameras`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wide` (default) | the drive: line of scrimmage, line to gain, the shadow plays, the first ring of stands | home sideline, 38 yd up, 82 yd back, 20 degrees; slides sideways with the ball while a play runs |
| `blimp` | the game: the whole field and the bowl | 300 yd up, 140 yd back, 30 degrees; holds still |

- **Team colors in the scene.** End zones keep the exact primary with white or alternate lettering and a white outline. Every path follows The Light Core Rule. The shadow plays are never team-colored.
- **Budget** (ui-architecture 6.1, 21.4). At most 150 draw calls and 300k triangles per frame, props at most 40 draw calls, 2 shadow-casting lights, bloom at half resolution, render on demand (0 frames while nothing moves), 60 fps at 1440x900 and 1920x1080 on Sam's laptop. The stadium is 5 draw calls (4 by day, 3 on a software rasterizer) and about 38k triangles with the field. Quality tiers drop the light shafts first, then the crowd, then bloom; a software rasterizer gets the low tier (DPR 1, no MSAA, no shadows, no bloom).
- **Room lights** (`html[data-ambient]`, the settings menu's switch). `dark` is the default. `room` (a lit meeting room or a projector) lifts the scene's exposure by 0.5 EV (`field/lighting.js` `ROOM_EV`, `exposureFor`), so a projector does not crush the bowl to mud; the HUD stays the same. The switch hands the change to the app first (a cancelable `sc:booth-ambient` event) and keeps `sc:ambient` itself, so it works either way.
- **The 2D fallback** (no WebGL2, a lost context, `?scene=2d`) is the same world seen from above: a `night-sky` ground, the striped field with team end zones, the same plays, shadow plays and plates in SVG (play-drawing.md 4b included), and the same HUD. Its two views carry the same names: Wide, the drive around the ball (the default, the shadow plays large enough to read), and Blimp, the whole field from straight above.

## Shapes

- **LED faces:** square corners (`rounded.led`, 0 px). A face is a rectangle on the 4 px pitch, and its edge is where the pixel grid ends; it has no border of its own.
- **Steel:** 2 px corners (`rounded.steel`), folded sheet metal, never more. Cabinets are rectangles with a caption plate on top and an 8 px frame around the face.
- **Hairline:** 1 px corners (`rounded.hairline`), only on parts a few pixels tall in the shared component CSS: the NEXT and LAST tags on the field's plates (`field-host.css` `.sc-label-tag`) and the neutral world's timeout pips (`scoreboard.css` `.sc-sb-to`, 14 x 4 px; this world draws timeouts as lamps). A 2 px corner would round a 4 px pip into a pill.
- **Lamps:** perfect circles, 10 px at 1440 and 12 px at 1920 (`rounded.lamp`), always paired with a word. With the ball's ring, the only round things on screen.
- **Pixel glyphs:** results (TD, INC, 1ST, FLAG, turnover), the verdicts (the check, the 2 and 3 of a top-3 rank, the X) and the hot arrows on a 9 by 9 pixel grid, lit in the LED palette; referee signal pictograms, drawn as pixel art, on the video board for touchdowns and turnovers only.
- **Strokes on the turf:** a play is an arc through the air or a straight flat line on the turf (play-drawing.md); a shadow play is the same shape, thin and dashed, with a small end mark (a run's arrowhead, a tick across a pass's landing spot). The 1-yard pixel grid of the seed's projection was replaced by these shapes in v1.3.
- **Refused shapes:** pills, rounded cards, cards inside cards, cut corners and slanted ends (broadcast graphics), icon tiles, circles used as decoration.

## Components

Every component is stadium hardware built from the three materials. States come from the attribute hooks in ui-architecture 2.7 and 5 (`data-phase`, `data-mode`, `aria-pressed`, `aria-expanded`, `data-verdict`, `data-tone`, `data-hot`), never from new markup.

### The top bar

A key the state can't use goes dark (Back and Reset at kickoff, Play and Forward at the end, Go Live at the live edge); a focused key that goes dark hands focus to the progress bar. While a request is in flight the row says so (`aria-busy`) and the strip dims a step; presses never stack (one in flight, the newest one waiting).
- **The progress bar** (`progress-bar`, a slider): an LED strip 20 px shorter than the ribbon (24 / 36 px), 0 at kickoff to the game's ESPN plays. The part played is lit amber cell by cell (a mask of the panel's pitch, never a glow). Ticks, each edged in the panel's black so it reads on the lit part too: a drive's start (warm white for his drives, dim for the opponent's), a quarter (full height, steel frame), a score (short, gold), and a score's tick appears only once play has passed it, so the bar never spoils the game (DC E5). The knob is a 4 px warm-white bar standing out of the strip, amber while dragged. Beside it the count, "104 / 183", in amber Big Shoulders at plate size (a ticking number under 40 px, so never Doto). A click jumps; a drag jumps where it is let go, one request. Focused, it keeps its own keys: Left and Right (and Up and Down) a drawn play, Page Up and Page Down the drive before and after, Home kickoff, End Go Live, Space play or pause.
- **Errors:** the watch's own words on an LED strip under the bar, over the bowl and never the board, beside a tally-red lamp.

### Video board (the card)

- **Cabinet:** `fascia-steel`, an 8 px frame at the sides and bottom, the `steel-edge` light-catch on top. 416 px wide at 1440 and 560 at 1920; as tall as its card (Layout). The LED face inside is 400 px wide at 1440 (measured).
- **One card, every section at once** (restructure-v2.md 3; `board.css`, `last-play.css`, `prediction.css`). It never switches between faces. Top to bottom, each section under its own painted plate (`caption-plate` type on raised steel, sticky while the cabinet scrolls), the plates parting the sections with no rule between them:
  1. **PREVIOUS PLAY** ("PREVIOUS PLAY · SNAP 34"; a call a flag wiped out has no snap number): his most recent call in the field's words, in board-line caps ("SHORT PASS LEFT · +11"); a touchdown or turnover leads with the referee's signal glyph, a touchdown in amber. Beside it (stepping under it when both don't fit) the verdict in Display caps at ribbon size, with its glyph, in the ribbon's lights: "TOP PICK · 19%" warm white with a check, "IN HIS TOP 3 · #2 INTERMEDIATE · 14%" amber with a 3, "MISS · #4 INTERMEDIATE · 10%" tally red with an X; a sack or a scramble, graded on its play type only, says so ("TOP PICK · PLAY TYPE ONLY"), since the tally under LAST 6 counts specific plays. When his call was not the top pick, a closing row: PREDICTED, the board's pick in Display caps (never cut) and its odds in Doto over an unlit 88%, in the pick's family tone. ESPN's own text rides on the play's tooltip. Absent before his first snap (no placeholder); it stays while the other team has the ball, and a new one scans in.
  2. **NEXT CALL**, plated with the caller ("SCHOTTENHEIMER'S NEXT CALL · SNAP 35"; with both sides tracked, the caller whose offense has the ball, Both sides tracked below; on 4th down "4TH DOWN · IF HE GOES FOR IT" in `line-to-gain-yellow`, its tooltip saying the model has no kick class). One of three faces: *the call* (below); *while he doesn't have the ball*, "TB HAS THE BALL" (or "TB KICKS OFF" before a kickoff) at call-name size, or, while ESPN names nobody with the ball (before kickoff, at the half), "When DAL gets the ball, his next call appears here." in `body`; this face has no number, so it draws no unlit 88% (an empty cell there read like a number to a first-time viewer); *at the final whistle*, FINAL: the score in segment digits as big as the call figure over their unlit 88s, the teams in scoreboard caption, and the TONIGHT'S SCORECARD key (a key with no window) that opens the Model Report, where the top bar's key goes. No SHADOW v LEAGUE number on the GameCast.
  3. **LAST 6**, under the plate "SCHOTTENHEIMER SO FAR": the label LAST 6, then his last six graded calls as 9 x 9 glyphs, oldest at the left so the newest sits at the right like the ribbon's newest message: a check (warm white), his rank in his top 3 as its own digit, 2 or 3 (amber), an X (tally red). A sack or a scramble has no specific play (the called route is unknown), so it is graded on its play type only: its check or X shows in `led-dim`, and a screen reader hears "Top pick (play type only)". Under them his night so far in label caps, "TOP PICK 32 OF 59 · IN HIS TOP 3 47 OF 59" (the scorecard's own counts of calls graded on the specific play, values in warm white); once a flag has wiped out a call or a call was graded on its play type only, a quiet line in `small` says how his calls split, counted from his own log: "59 calls: 55 snaps and 4 wiped out by flags", "38 calls: 34 snaps and 4 wiped out; 5 graded on play type only" (one line: with the play type said, "by flags" goes), "6 calls: 2 graded on play type only". It stays hidden while his calls, his snaps and the tally's count agree. Absent before his first graded call.
- **The call's face, top to bottom:** the call name (`call-name`, warm white, one line); the figure row: the percentage (`led-call-figure`, warm white over an unlit `88%`, its `%` from "ULS LED Pct") and, at the right, the tilt tag (an LED tag in label caps, G4-6: "TILTS PASS", "LEANS RUN", "LIKE THE LEAGUE"; amber for a tilt or a lean, `led-dim` for like the league. The server's flag compares his pass rate here with the league's, so "no edge" always meant "calls it like the league", never "the model has no edge"); the then-line ("THEN" in `led-dim`, then #2 and #3 one to a row, names warm white in sentence case, percentages amber); the pass/run split as one LED bar (pass cells warm white, run cells amber) with the league as a 2-cell warm-white notch above and below the bar, and its words under it ("Ball thrown or dropback 46%" at the left, "Handed off or kept 54%" in amber at the right; the league's share joins them when the card opens); five family rows: name (`label`, `led-dim`), an LED bar of lit cells in the family's tone to the family's percentage and unlit cells to 100%, the league notch, his percentage, and the hot arrow as a pixel triangle (`data-hot="up"` lit in the family tone, `"down"` in `led-dim`).
- **States:** the top pick, a then-line item, a family row (its most likely play) or a specific play under the pointer or focus gets a 2 px warm-white frame and lights its shadow play on the turf (`highlightGhost`); nothing on the card is pressed or picked, and nothing on it enters the game. A new prediction scans in (400 ms). The one verdict on the card is PREVIOUS PLAY's: NEXT CALL never grades itself.
- **Open:** while the pointer or keyboard focus is inside the board, the card keeps its width (it never reaches over the field) and grows down, scrolling inside its cabinet once it meets the lower ribbon, and scans in the rest: every specific play under its family as a button (bar scaled to the top pick, his percentage, the hot arrow), the why lines in sentence case (pass points warm white, run points amber), the opening-script note and the legend in `small`. Closed, those wait visually hidden, so Tab and screen readers still reach them. The whole card speaks one sentence per change in one live region.

### Lower ribbon: drive chart and win probability

### Keys, lamps, lamp switches and LED inputs

- **Key** (`key`): a raised steel cap, 2 px corners, 1 px `steel-edge` light-catch on top; at its left a square LED window carrying the key letter (`key-letter`, warm white), then its name (`label`, `led-dim`). ### Both sides tracked

With both sides tracked (restructure-v2.md 11 decision 4) the game has two play-callers, each with his own model, memory and scorecard, and the stadium follows the ball:

- **The field** keeps the game's frame: the home team attacks left to right as a lone tracked offense does, the other team right to left. Before every snap of either offense its caller's two shadow plays go up, drawn from that offense's side with its own line to gain.
- **The video board** follows the offense with the ball and names its caller wherever "he" would be unclear: "ROBINSON'S NEXT CALL · SNAP 12", "ROBINSON'S PREVIOUS PLAY", "4TH DOWN · IF ROBINSON GOES FOR IT"; its LAST 6 and night so far are his.
- **The top bar** names both callers; the ticker lights both offenses' plays warm white with their verdicts. The drive chart, the progress bar's drive ticks and the win probability keep the game's frame: the home team's in warm white, the other's in amber or dim.
- **The Model Report** gets its switch (Layout): the boards of one caller (his night so far, scorecard, hit rate, surprises, tendencies, memory, every call) show the caller picked, their plates naming him ("ROBINSON'S SCORECARD"); win probability, drives and team stats are the whole game's.

### The analyst

One chat in two places (docs/design/analyst-ui.md): the drawer on the GameCast (`ask-drawer`; Layout gives its box) and the last board of the Model Report. Both are a steel cabinet with an 8 px frame round LED faces, flat like every panel.

- **The plate:** ASK THE ANALYST in plate caps, a lamp and the analyst's state in words: unlit while checking; lamp green READY; amber WARMING UP, BUSY or ANSWERING; tally red NOT READY, OFFLINE or NOT SET UP. In the drawer, the Esc key (Close).
- **The note:** an LED strip under the plate when the state has something to say, in the server's own words when the analyst isn't ready ("The analyst needs Ollama running with gpt-oss:20b. Start Ollama, then ask again.").
- **The intro:** "Ask about tonight's game, his calls or how the model works.", what it answers from (this game's plays and the model's own numbers, only up to the play on screen, on this computer), and four suggested questions as rows of the board, each a button that asks at once. It shows until the first question; from 56 rem wide (the Model Report) it stays beside the conversation.
- **The conversation,** on an LED face read like a stadium's captioning board: the question in amber, the answer in warm white, both in sentence case at board-line size; "THINKING…" before anything arrives, "LOADING THE MODEL, ABOUT 10 S…" on a cold model, and a lookup at work as a dim caps line beside an amber lamp ("LOOKING THROUGH THE PLAYS…"); each play it cites an LED key in a `steel-frame` border reading the clock in amber ("Q3 2:52"); an error a tally-red lamp beside the server's words, with what had arrived kept above it; STOPPED. for a stopped answer, and ASK AGAIN on the newest failed or stopped one. The face follows new words while the reader is at its end and stays put once they scroll up.
- **The desk:** an LED input (amber caret, up to six lines, 500 characters) and the ENTER key, ASK (STOP while answering, at the same width, so nothing jumps); "Shift+Enter for a new line." in `small`, and the count ("412 / 500") in amber from 400 characters.
- **Text only:** questions and answers render as text, never markup (an answer can quote ESPN's play text); one polite announcement per finished answer, never per word.
- **Cites:** on the GameCast a cite key seeks the game to that play (paused; in a live game that leaves Go Live until Esc). On the Model Report it scrolls that call's row into view clear of the sticky top bar and lights it for 2.4 s (switching callers first when both sides are tracked), or lights the play's drive on the drive chart when it has no row.
- **Keys:** Enter asks and Shift+Enter starts a new line. Inside the drawer no page shortcut fires; its Escape closes it and gives focus back to the ASK key without going live.

### Model Report boards

Every board on the Model Report (`report-board`) is the video board's hardware hung on a page: a steel cabinet (an 8 px frame, 2 px corners) around an LED face with its unlit pixels, under a painted plate (`caption-plate` type on raised steel with its light-catch). - **His night so far** ("SCHOTTENHEIMER SO FAR"): the top-pick and top-3 tallies in amber Doto at call-figure size over their unlit 88s ("32 OF 59 CALLS"); his last six as glyphs in small LED windows; and the call track, every call in order, one bar per call: a socket of 12 unlit cells lit from the bottom to the model's chance on the play he called (a cell is 5 points, so a full bar is 60%), in its verdict's light, a gap where his drive changes, the quarter under the bar that starts it, and the number of each of his five biggest surprises over its bar. A legend names the three lights. Each bar says its call in words on its tooltip and to a screen reader.
- **His scorecard** ("SCHOTTENHEIMER'S SCORECARD"): the app's scorecard at full size. Every SHADOW v LEAGUE pair the scorecard computes, each with its short caption (G4-3: "RUN/PASS RIGHT", "PLAY IN TOP 3", "PASS VS RUN LOG LOSS", "PLAY TYPE LOG LOSS", "SPECIFIC PLAY LOG LOSS"): a painted caption over the SHADOW and LEAGUE windows in amber Doto (`led-readout`) with a small "V" between them, the leader lit and the trailing value dimmed, an amber leader lamp beside the side that leads (none when even); the basis said once ("HIS MODEL V LEAGUE BASELINE · 59 SNAPS"), or, when the pairs count different snaps (a sack or a scramble is graded on pass or run and its play type, never on a specific play), each pair's own count on a line under its readouts, clear of the next pair; then the scorecard's groups and the note "Lower log loss is better. One game is a pipeline test, not proof." There is no league top-pick count and no league tilt count, so no such cell exists on any board, at any size.
- **Hit rate by down and distance:** an LED table in dim caps heads and steel seams: 1st; 2nd and 3rd short (1 to 3 yds), medium (4 to 6) and long (7+); 4th; then all downs. A row's calls stand as a strip of LED cells in their verdicts' lights (top picks, then the rest of his top 3, then misses) beside the count, then his top picks, his top 3 and the league baseline's top 3 on the same snaps, a lamp lit on the side that leads. Counts only, never a percentage of a handful.
- **His biggest surprises:** the five calls the model gave the lowest chance, numbered in Doto windows (the numbers the call track wears): when and where ("SNAP 47 · Q4 5:35 · 1ST & 10 AT TB 18"), what he called with the model's chance on it and its rank ("#9 of 14"), and the model's own pick with its chance.
- **His tendencies:** the app's LED forest plot (pass terms warm white, run terms amber, filled pixels when the 90% interval excludes zero, hollow otherwise; axis words "more run" and "more pass"), his play-type mix against expected, and the held-out check.
- **In-game memory:** the snaps where the model tilted his call away from the league: an amber TILTS PASS or TILTS RUN tag, when and where, what his habits and the in-game memory each did to his pass chance in points, the memory's parts, what he called, and whether the tilt came true beside its lamp; over them, the tilts that came true and the snap memory switches on after.
- **Win probability:** the app's LED line, large, its readout in Doto over an unlit 88%.
- **Drives:** the app's drive chart; a drive of his scrolls to its first call in the table below (with both sides tracked, a drive of either caller, switching to his boards first), an untracked offense's drive rests disabled.
- **Team stats:** the app's box score as an LED table.
- **Every call v the model:** an LED table, oldest first: a row for each of his drives (its result and summary in the drive chart's words), then each call: #, time, situation, his top 3 with their chances (the one he called marked, in words too), what he called with its chance (a NO PLAY mark when a flag wiped it out), its rank, and the verdict with its glyph.
- **Ask the analyst:** the analyst's chat (The analyst, above) as the last board, spanning both columns.
- **Empty:** before his first snap each board of his calls shows one dim LED tag, "NO CALLS YET: HIS FIRST SNAP HASN'T HAPPENED."

### Toasts, connection banner, field legend, boot screen

- **Toast** (`toast`): an LED strip that steps in above the lower ribbon, centered on the field's free area (at most 560 px wide at 1440, 720 at 1920): an amber lamp (info) or tally-red lamp (error) and the message in `body`, sentence case. Info holds 2.6 s (FI 4.5); an error holds 8 s, and longer while it is pointed at or focused, until it is read or dismissed (Esc). `role="status"`.
- **Connection banner** (`conn-banner`): a full-width LED strip directly under the top bar: a yellow lamp and "RECONNECTING…", or a tally-red lamp and "CAN'T REACH THE APP SERVER. RETRYING IN 4 S.", with a RETRY NOW key at its right end. It covers the bowl, never the board. On the Model Report it hangs under that page's top bar.
- **Field legend:** "Ball shown mid-field; ESPN gives no hash." on a small LED tag at the bottom left of the free area, in `small`, `led-dim`, always present. Above it, while his next call is up, the style key (play-drawing.md 4b): "LAST PLAY", "NEXT 1", "NEXT 2", each with its stroke's swatch.
- **Boot screen:** the stadium before the lights come on: the `night-sky` ground and a single centered LED board, "LOADING THE GAME…" in ribbon type; failed and restart states are the same board with a tally-red lamp, the copy, and `bash start.sh` in an LED window.

### The shadow plays (signature)

The binding rules are play-drawing.md sections 4 and 4b (Sam, 2026-10-10: "make it clear which ones are predictions and which ones are previous plays and make sure they don't overlap on each other or the ball").

- **When:** before every caller snap, while a prediction is up (`data-phase="presnap"`): with both sides tracked, every snap of either offense, each drawn from its own side. An untracked offense's snaps get none (it has no model). In a watched game the call and its shadows stay up about 2.6 s before each of his snaps at 4x and 1.8 s at 10x (data contract E3, `anim-queue.js` `CALL_RESERVE_MS`).
- **Sequence:** the stadium lights dip to 80% (300 ms; `look.world.uls.dip`) → his top 2 plays fade onto the turf (400 ms) as the board's call scans in, and his previous play turns steel and dims → they hold until the snap → at the snap the lights come back (300 ms), the shadows fade out (300 ms) and the real play draws solid with its label. With the live OFF setting there is no dip; under reduced motion everything is instant and there is no dip.
- **Shape (section 4):** his top 2 specific plays by probability, in the same shape language as real plays: a pass an arc to the middle lane at its bucket's typical air yards (short 6, intermediate 14, deep 25; play-action the same), a screen a short arc to the flat on the wider side, an RPO handoff a line and an RPO throw a short arc, a run a straight line on the turf about 4 yd long angled to its gap (inside straight ahead, off-tackle about 4 yd wide, outside about 9). Every stroke starts 1.5 yd past the ball, never on it.
- **Look (4b.1 and 4b.2):** thin, never chunky: a dashed line 2.5 px wide at the 1440x900 Wide camera (scaled with the canvas height, 0.8x to 1.4x), dashes 0.7 yd on and 0.4 yd off, each over a thin `night-sky` casing (55%, 2.2 times its width) so the dashes hold over white paint, and a small end mark at most (a run's arrowhead, a tick across a pass's landing spot). No filled lane, block, broad arrow or landing ring. Color-coded by rank, never by team or family: his #1 in `led-amber`, his #2 in `led-warm-white`, a little fainter (line opacity 95% and 83%; on 4th down, "if he goes", 83% and 75%). Never solid.
- **Separated (4b.3):** when their natural shapes come within 3 yd of each other (a short pass and an inside run, an inside and an outside run, two short passes), they fan out to lanes at least 4 yd apart: passes take the far lanes, so an arc rises away from a run on the turf instead of over it, and the deeper pass flies the farther lane. Where his previous play's stroke lies in their way ahead of the ball, they take lanes clear of it too (`shadow-lanes.js`).
- **The previous play (4b.2):** while they are up, his last play stays as a solid stroke in `last-play-slate` with a `steel-edge` edge, dimmed (60%), never amber or warm white.
- **Plates (4b.2, 4b.4):** every plate says what it is. "NEXT 1 · Inside run (A/B gap) · 21%" and "NEXT 2 · PA short (0–9) · 16%" (on 4th down "NEXT 1 · If he goes · ..."), each with a tab in its stroke's color carrying the tag (Display caps, `led-panel` letters) and an edge in that color; "LAST · Short pass left · +11" with its verdict on a second line ("TOP PICK · 19%", "MISS · #4 Intermediate · 10%") in a muted slate plate. Plates are placed in screen space after projection (`presnap-layout.js`, `label-place.js`): none on the ball, a stroke or another plate, the LAST plate at its play's start or along its path, never at its end where the ball now rests, and a thin leader line back to its spot when a plate had to move.
- **Style key:** a small LED tag at the bottom left of the free area, above the field legend, while his next call is up: "LAST PLAY", "NEXT 1", "NEXT 2", each with its stroke's swatch.
- **Linked:** pointing at or focusing a play on the card lights that play's shadow (95%) and dims the others (to 45% of their own); a play outside his top 2 gets a shadow while pointed at, on its own side, outside them, so his top 2 never move. The shadows never change color with the verdict.
- **Acceptance (4b.5):** `tests/e2e/test_shadow_clarity.py` measures all 59 Dallas pre-snaps of the saved TB at DAL game at 1440x900 and 1920x1080 (1512x982 on a sample) on the default pre-snap camera: no plate on a plate, the ball or a stroke; his two predictions at least 3 yd apart (4 once fanned), each starting at least 1.5 yd from the ball; the previous play tagged LAST, solid and in its own color, the predictions tagged NEXT 1 and NEXT 2, dashed, in two others. The 2D field gets the same checks on a sample.

### Plays on the field

The binding grammar is docs/design/play-drawing.md; in short:

- **Every play starts on the line of scrimmage**, on the center line between the hashes (ESPN gives no hash). No dropback, handoff mesh or fake. Kicks start on the line too (DECIDED, v1.4); a field goal's label keeps the true distance ("FG 41 yds · good").
- **Shapes:** a completed pass is an arc to the catch (a small catch dot), then a straight line on the turf for the yards after the catch; an incompletion is the arc ending in an X; an interception the arc to the spot, an INT mark, then the return in the defense's color; a run, scramble or kneel a straight flat line angled to its gap with its yards; a sack a line backward in a loss color; punts, kickoffs and field goals arcs at their true length with their result marks; a touchdown ends in the end zone with a TD mark.
- **Flags:** a play wiped out by a flag draws dimmed and dashed (38%), a flag at the line, and the ball walks to the enforced spot; a flag with no play is the flag at the line and the walk.
- **The label:** every play carries one, near its end and never over the ball: "Deep pass left · +21", "Inside run (up the middle) · +4", "Punt · 53 yds · out of bounds", "No play · defensive offside on TB", "Flag · false start on DAL · −5" (a true minus sign). Labels are text, never HTML.
- **Paths:** a light core (`light-cone-white`) with the team color on its edge (The Light Core Rule): arcs 4.5 px, lines on the turf 0.62 yd, marks 2.4 yd; an estimate (an incompletion's "short" or "deep" depth, a loose ball's runback) is dashed, a measured fact solid.
- **Persistence:** the last play stays until the next snap draws; once his next call is up it turns `last-play-slate` and dims to 60% under its LAST plate (The shadow plays). The drive's earlier plays stay as residue (16%) until the drive ends.
- **Ball:** the football at 2.2x scale with a tracer head and a spark trail in the team color that fades in 400 ms; the head is the only blooming thing on the field.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** keep a stadium behind every pixel: the `night-sky` ground and the lit bowl at night, `day-sky` and sun in the afternoon.
- **Do** make every LED surface show its unlit pixels (4 px grid of `led-unlit-pixel`) and every digit cell its unlit 8s.
- **Do** keep panels opaque and flat: LED black or painted steel, a 2 px light-catch on top, 1 px seams.
- **Do** paint the scoreboard band in the offense's exact ESPN primary and repaint it on every possession change.
- **Do** set the call's name on one line in Big Shoulders Display (40 px at 1440, 54 px at 1920) and its percentage in Doto (64 px, 88 px); the call is the brightest, largest readout on screen.
- **Do** put a light core in every drawn path and carry team colors only on edges and fills.
- **Do** draw every estimate dashed and every measured fact solid; draw the shadow plays thin and dashed, NEXT 1 amber and NEXT 2 warm white, never solid, and the previous play in slate, tagged LAST.
- **Do** show SHADOW v LEAGUE on the Model Report only, and only the pairs the scorecard computes.
- **Do** show every section of the card at once (PREVIOUS PLAY, NEXT CALL, LAST 6), and drop decoration before shrinking type to fit it.
- **Do** print every key's keyboard key on its cap, and keep the keys to the spec's: Space, Left, Right, 1 to 4, V, Esc.
- **Do** step LED text in at 12 fps and hold it; fade the shadow plays and scan the call in over 400 ms; snap digits; make every motion instant under reduced motion.
- **Do** honor the live-game light-dip OFF setting, and check every screen at 1440x900, 1512x982, 1920x1080 and 390x844 with real copy, including the longest call name ("RPO, handed off or kept").
- **Do** keep the page's shortcuts out of the game picker and the analyst's drawer: they keep their own keys, and their Escape closes them without going live.
- **Do** render the analyst's answers, ESPN's text and every label as text, never markup.

### Don't:

- **Don't** use a black or near-black flat UI ground, a neon hue, or a glowing edge: that is the dark analytics dashboard this world was chosen against.
- **Don't** give anything a glow, halo, bloom or pulse unless it is a physical fixture (tower lamps, LED fascia in the bowl) or the ball's tracer.
- **Don't** use `box-shadow` (the focus ring's gutter is the one exception), `backdrop-filter`, glass, blur, decorative gradients, pills, rounded cards or nested cards anywhere in the HUD.
- **Don't** set Doto below 40 px, type ▲ ▼ in Doto, or put a ticking number of 40 px or more in Big Shoulders (no tabular figures); a smaller one, like the progress count, sits in a fixed-width cell.
- **Don't** put input into the game back on the GameCast (a keypad, flag panel, shorthand bar, retag keys, Undo, an editable situation), or a drawer, a stat board, SHADOW v LEAGUE numbers or a Tight camera (restructure-v2.md 1 and 4).
- **Don't** let the camera move on its own: no push-in at the snap, no rise or drift between drives.
- **Don't** show a score on the progress bar before play has passed it.
- **Don't** truncate, abbreviate or ellipsize the call's name.
- **Don't** put team colors on LED (text, bars, glows; a team's own logo image is the one exception) or use a team color as the only signal of a path.
- **Don't** invent a statistic: no league top-pick count, no league tilt count, and no more precision than the scorecard shows (whole percentages, 3 decimals for pass vs run log loss, 2 for the others).
- **Don't** draw players, routes, formations, a dropback or a handoff mesh, or a side for a pass prediction: a shadow play is a shape from the line of scrimmage, nothing more.
- **Don't** crawl ribbon text continuously, crossfade boards, or animate paint.
- **Don't** let any panel cover the line of scrimmage, the line to gain or the shadow plays: the camera's safe area keeps them in the free field, the analyst's drawer included.
- **Don't** offer a side the picker can't open: a side whose play-caller has no built model is disabled, with the reason from the models' index.
